# The featured years: what the app says, and why

Generated 2026-11-10 by `scripts/review_featured.py`. Do not edit by hand.

For 1971, 1985, 1997, 2008 and 2022, every statement the app makes is listed with the evidence behind it.
Quotes were chosen by a person and are checked automatically to appear in their sources; the reading of a
quote (does it support the statement?) is the reviewer's judgement, recorded here for anyone to check.
Primary sources are records of the bodies that acted; secondary ones (Wikipedia, Federal Reserve History
essays) are marked. Leaders' terms are checked separately (docs/leaders-verification.md).

Event dates and exchange-rate dates differ: rates are weekly observations (Fridays), and the app stops the
year at the first observation on or after an event and names that week.

## 1971

### Events

- **1971-05-10 The Mark floats** (reviewed): When currency markets reopen on 10 May, West Germany lets the Mark float instead of holding its fixed rate.
  - The Mark floats from 10 May 1971: "When foreign exchange markets reopened on May 10, the Mark was allowed to float" ([Office of the Historian, U.S. Department of State](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v03/d151), primary)
- **1971-08-15 The dollar-gold break** (reviewed): The United States suspends the convertibility of the dollar into gold. The fixed-rate system begins to break apart.
  - Date of the address: "Address to the Nation Outlining a New Economic Policy: "The Challenge of Peace." August 15, 1971" ([The American Presidency Project (UC Santa Barbara)](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/address-the-nation-outlining-new-economic-policy-the-challenge-peace), primary)
  - The dollar's convertibility into gold is suspended: "suspend temporarily the convertibility of the dollar into gold or other reserve assets" ([The American Presidency Project (UC Santa Barbara)](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/address-the-nation-outlining-new-economic-policy-the-challenge-peace), primary)
  - The fixed-rate system begins to break apart: "This was the end of the Bretton Woods international monetary system" ([Ministry of Finance Japan, Policy Research Institute](https://www.mof.go.jp/english/pri/publication/policy_1972-1990/Chapter1-3.pdf), primary)
- **1971-08-28 Japan lets the yen float** (reviewed): Two weeks after the US move, Japan stops holding the yen at 360 per dollar and lets it float.
  - Japan floats on 28 August 1971: "so that world currencies provisionally went into the floating exchange rate system (on August 28, for Japan)" ([Ministry of Finance Japan, Policy Research Institute](https://www.mof.go.jp/english/pri/publication/policy_1972-1990/Chapter1-3.pdf), primary)
  - The 360-yen rate ends: "The exchange rate of 360 yen per dollar, which started in 1949, also ended" ([Ministry of Finance Japan, Policy Research Institute](https://www.mof.go.jp/english/pri/publication/policy_1972-1990/Chapter1-3.pdf), primary)
- **1971-12-18 The Smithsonian Agreement** (reviewed): Meeting at the Smithsonian Institution in Washington, the Group of Ten agrees on new fixed rates: the dollar is devalued against gold, and the yen is set at 308 per dollar.
  - Agreement on 18 December 1971 in Washington: "an agreement was made at the Smithsonian Meeting (in Washington) on December 18" ([Ministry of Finance Japan, Policy Research Institute](https://www.mof.go.jp/english/pri/publication/policy_1972-1990/Chapter1-3.pdf), primary)
  - Date of the announcement: "Remarks Announcing a Monetary Agreement Following a Meeting of the Group of Ten December 18, 1971" ([The American Presidency Project (UC Santa Barbara)](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/remarks-announcing-monetary-agreement-following-meeting-the-group-ten), primary)
  - The dollar is devalued against gold: "agreement by the United States to propose to the Congress a suitable means for devaluing the dollar in terms of gold" ([The American Presidency Project (UC Santa Barbara)](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/remarks-announcing-monetary-agreement-following-meeting-the-group-ten), primary)
  - The yen is set at 308 per dollar: "The Smithsonian Agreement in December 1971 devalued the yen parity by 16.88% to ¥308" ([Ministry of Finance Japan, Policy Research Institute](https://www.mof.go.jp/english/pri/publication/policy_1972-1990/Chapter1-3.pdf), primary)
